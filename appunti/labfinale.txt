From the fusion results we can see that all the fusions that we 
considered performs better than the logistic regression model and the SVM 
with RBF kernel. We can thou see that the GMM already performed similarly 
to the fusion models (even better than the fusion models).
Setting pT for the prior weighted L.R. for the fusion creates the best fused models


The GMM is the most stable and well-calibrated model, with very low Mis￾scalibration Loss values in all prior scenarios suggesting that the raw 
scores are already well aligned with the posterior probabilities.


The SVM model shows a strong miscalibration at pT = 0.1 and pT = 0.9, with 
a Mis-Calibration Loss of 0.128 and 0.272 respectively. This indicates 
that the scores are not directly interpretable as probabilities, especially 
in the unbalanced cases.

The quadratic logistic regression (LR) model has an intermediate behavior: it 
presents a moderate Mis-Calibration Loss at pT = 0.1 (0.014) and pT = 0.9 
(0.039), but very low at pT = 0.5.


Calibration via logistic regression significantly improved the SVM, 
especially for the extreme prior values. 
In the case of GMM, calibration had little or no impact, confirming that 
this model generates well-calibrated scores natively. 
For the LR model, surprisingly, calibration did not improve the actDCF 
significantly; in some cases, such as at pT = 0.1, the calibrated value 
even slightly worsened (from 0.524 to 0.538), suggesting a possible 
overfitting or instability of the calibration process. 

GMM is naturally well-calibrated, making it suitable for a variety of 
applications without the need for post-processing. 
SVM benefits strongly from calibration, especially in cases with unbalanced 
priors. 
LR, despite being a probabilistic model, may require more careful 
calibration, perhaps with regularization or alternative techniques, to 
avoid post-treatment worsening.
